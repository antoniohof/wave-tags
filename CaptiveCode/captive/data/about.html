THE BACKROOMS NETWORK

Antonio Hofmeister Ribeiro & Liudmila Savelyeva, 2025

The Backrooms Network (2025) transforms abandoned Wi-Fi signals into a space for anonymous, collective expression. Visitors connect to local offline networks, leaving digital traces that form a ghostly, participatory archive—making the invisible architecture of connectivity visible, tactile, and communal.

Inspired by the disorienting, liminal spaces of Stanisław Lem's Return from the Stars—where the protagonist returns to an Earth rendered alien by societal and technological evolution—"The Backrooms Network" is an intervention that breathes digital life into the post-capitalist shell of the exhibition venue.

This project draws a conceptual parallel to Louise Drulhe's "Critical Atlas of the Internet," which frames the digital realm not as an abstract cloud, but as a tangible, spatially complex, and politically charged territory with its own topography, gravity, and frontiers.

The stalagmite-like objects stand in the dim light, pulsing softly with a quiet energy. They feel like relics from a lost, post-digital world — both mysterious artifacts and working devices. Their glow and semi-transparent surfaces recall the crystal-books in Stanisław Lem’s science-fiction novel Return from the Stars (1991), where knowledge is stored in prisms and read by special machines. In the same way, these forms seem to hold traces of communication, crystallizing messages and technology into bridges between the physical and the digital.

Visitors are invited to connect to these local, offline Wi-Fi networks. Upon connection, they can anonymously leave digital "tags"—short messages that can range from poetic fragments and political manifestos to simple names or cryptic symbols. The resulting constellation of messages forms a decentralized, local mesh network—a ghostly, participatory archive of traces and a local anonymous publishing platform.

How It Works

When you submit a message, it becomes a Wi-Fi network name visible to anyone scanning for networks in this area. These digital breadcrumbs are invisible to the naked eye but discoverable by anyone scanning the electromagnetic spectrum, turning the act of network discovery into an act of reading.

Your digital traces will exist both in the local mesh network and in the broader digital archive, creating a bridge between site-specific installation and global accessibility.

About the Artists

Antonio Hofmeister Ribeiro & Liudmila Savelyeva

This work builds upon the original "Wave Tags" project, reimagined for The Backrooms exhibition as an exploration of how digital infrastructure can activate abandoned spaces and create new forms of collective expression.
