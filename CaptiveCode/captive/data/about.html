THE BACKROOMS NETWORK

Welcome to the WiFi-scape of an abandoned office building. Here, in the invisible dimension of electromagnetic spectrum, new forms of collective publishing emerge.

Inspired by the disorienting, liminal spaces of Stanislaw Lem's Return from the Stars - where the protagonist returns to an Earth rendered alien by societal and technological evolution - "The Backrooms Network" is an intervention that breathes digital life into the post-capitalist shell of the exhibition venue.

This project draws a conceptual parallel to Louise Drulhe's "Critical Atlas of the Internet", which frames the digital realm not as an abstract cloud, but as a tangible, spatially complex, and politically charged territory with its own topography, gravity, and frontiers.

Just as Drulhe reveals the "porous surface" of the internet that absorbs our metadata and the "steep slope" that funnels us towards centralized platforms, "The Backrooms Network" seeks to activate the invisible, forgotten layer of the physical space: its abandoned WiFi spectrum. We propose to transform this spectral, non-place into a site of collective, anonymous expression. Building upon our earlier "Wave Tags" prototype - a subversive, hidden device for decentralized street communication - we deploy a series of five reimagined, visible devices throughout the Backrooms. These are not hidden, they are speculative hyper-objects, glowing beacons crafted from phosphorescent materials that pulse gently in the dim light. They serve as both haunting relics of a fictional, failed digital infrastructure and as functional portals for participation.

Visitors are invited to connect to these local, offline WiFi networks. Upon connection, they can anonymously leave digital "tags" - short messages that can range from poetic fragments and political manifestos to simple names or cryptic symbols.

The resulting constellation of messages forms a decentralized, local mesh network - a ghostly, participatory archive of traces and a local anonymous publishing platform. These digital breadcrumbs will be invisible to the naked eye but discoverable by anyone scanning for WiFi networks in the vicinity, turning the act of network discovery into an act of reading.

The Backrooms Network is thus a spatial commentary. It asks: What happens when we treat the invisible architecture of connectivity not as a corporate service, but as a public, malleable space? By making the network visible, tactile, and participatory, we challenge the centralized "gravity" of the modern internet (as described by Drulhe) and reclaim a sliver of the digital realm for anonymous, local, and collective expression. It is an act of digital hauntology, where the ghosts of users past and present whisper their presence through the very airwaves of an abandoned world.

How It Works

When you submit a message, it becomes a WiFi network name visible to anyone scanning for networks in this area. Your trace propagates through the electromagnetic spectrum, creating a temporary constellation of thoughts, feelings, and expressions floating in the space.
